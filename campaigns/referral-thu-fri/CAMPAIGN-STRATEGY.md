# Campaign Strategy — Filling Thursdays & Fridays

How to get the most out of the referral campaign, and what has to happen around it for the goal to actually be met.

Read `SEND-PLAN.md` for the mechanics of sending and `REFERRAL-SOP.md` for running the referral program in the salon. This document is the strategy that wraps both.

---

## 1. The goal, in numbers

"Fill Thursdays and Fridays" needs a number attached or you can't tell whether it worked.

| | |
|---|---|
| Opening hours | 10am–6pm, two days = **16 bookable hours/week** |
| Typical mix | Colour 2.5–3h · cut & blow wave ~1h · consultation 20min |
| Realistic capacity | **3–4 appointments per day → 6–8 per week** |
| Over an 8-week campaign | **≈ 48–64 appointment slots** |

That is the real target. Not "some referrals" — roughly fifty to sixty-five filled appointments over two months.

## 2. The honest gap

The referral campaign, run well, delivers **15–30 of those slots**. On a ~300-person warm list, 2–5% referring is a normal result; add the rebookings that email 5 triggers and the direct bookings from emails 1–3.

That is somewhere between a quarter and a half of the target. **The referral campaign alone will not fill Thursdays and Fridays.** It is the right centrepiece — referred clients are the most valuable kind, with materially higher retention than any other acquisition source — but it needs three other levers running alongside it.

This isn't a reason to change the campaign. It's a reason not to stop at the campaign.

---

## 3. The five levers, ranked

Ranked by *speed × yield for this specific goal*, not by how impressive they sound.

### Lever 1 — Rebook at the chair *(biggest, fastest, free)*

Every client who sits in the chair Monday to Wednesday is a Thursday or Friday booking waiting to happen. They already come. They already pay. They just need to be offered the day.

If Sha sees 15–20 clients a week and converts half onto a Thu/Fri next appointment, that's **7–10 slots a week** — more than the entire referral campaign delivers in eight weeks.

> **This is the single highest-leverage action in the whole plan, and it costs nothing.**

The script is in §5. Do this from day one, before a single email is sent.

### Lever 2 — Lapsed client reactivation

A salon list of any age carries 30–50% lapsed clients — people who haven't been in for six months or more and simply drifted. They already know and trust Sha; there's no persuasion needed, only a reason and a prompt.

`06-lapsed-reactivation` does this. It carries **no discount deliberately** — discounting lapsed clients teaches them to wait for offers, and devalues the work. The reason to return is the new capacity and the free consultation.

Expect **5–10% to book** from a well-written reactivation email. On 100 lapsed contacts, that's 5–10 appointments.

### Lever 3 — The referral campaign

Emails 1, 2, 3 and 5, plus the in-salon ask. Slowest to pay off, but delivers the highest-quality clients and compounds — referred clients refer others at 2–3× the normal rate. Build it to keep, not for a burst.

### Lever 4 — Google Business Profile

For a local salon this is the highest-intent traffic that exists: someone typing "hairdresser Camberwell" is ready to book today. It is free and most salons neglect it.

Three actions, all one-off:
1. **Update the opening hours** to reflect the new Thursday/Friday availability. If the profile still shows old hours, Google is actively hiding available capacity.
2. **Post the Thu/Fri availability** as a Google Post (they surface in local results and expire after 7 days — repost monthly).
3. **Ask for reviews.** See §5 for the ask. Review count and recency are direct local-ranking factors, and the site currently has *placeholder* testimonials that need replacing with real ones anyway.

### Lever 5 — Instagram

Instagram supports the other levers; it rarely drives bookings on its own at this scale. Its job is to make the referral easy — when someone is told "book with Sha," the first thing they do is look her up. If the profile is stale, the referral dies there.

> **No account is currently referenced.** The old `@infusion_hair_` handle has been retired and removed from every email. This lever is on hold until a current handle is confirmed; if there isn't an active account, skip Lever 5 entirely and put the effort into Levers 1 and 2, which matter more anyway.

Minimum viable: post the Thu/Fri news once, keep 6–9 recent results visible, put the booking link in bio. That's it. Don't build a content strategy to fill two days.

## 4. The eight-week calendar

| Week | Do this |
|---|---|
| **0 — Prep** | Google hours updated. Assets uploaded. Resend domain authenticated. Test send checked on phone + desktop. Tracker open. **Start rebooking at the chair immediately.** |
| **1** | **Tue:** Email 1 (announcement) to active clients. Begin the verbal referral ask in the salon. |
| **2** | **Tue:** Email 6 (lapsed reactivation) to 6+ month lapsed segment. Post Thu/Fri news to Instagram + Google Post. |
| **3** | **Wed:** Email 2 (reminder) to non-referrers. Resend Email 1 to non-openers with the alternate subject line. |
| **4** | Review tracker. Text any referrer whose friend has attended but who hasn't rebooked. Begin asking happy clients for Google reviews. |
| **5** | **Tue:** Email 3 (capacity close). Instagram: post a recent Thu/Fri result. |
| **6** | Quiet week. Keep rebooking at the chair. Send email 5 as rewards become due. |
| **7** | Review: how many Thu/Fri slots filled? If under half, the constraint is almost certainly rebooking discipline, not the emails — see §7. |
| **8** | Measure against the target. Decide what becomes permanent (§6). |

Emails 4 and 5 run continuously on trigger, not on this calendar.

## 5. Ready-to-use scripts

### The rebooking ask *(Lever 1 — use this every single appointment)*

At the end, while they're happy and looking in the mirror, **before** they reach the counter:

> "Do you want me to put your next one in now? I've got Thursdays and Fridays open at the moment — they're my quietest days, so you'd have the place to yourself."

Then offer two specific times, never an open question:

> "I could do Thursday the 14th at 10:30, or Friday the 22nd at 2 — which works better?"

Two named options convert dramatically better than "when suits you?", which asks the client to do the work and usually ends in "I'll text you."

**If they hesitate:** *"No pressure — shall I pencil Thursday and you confirm next week?"*

### The verbal referral ask *(use once, lightly, at the right moment)*

When they're pleased with the result:

> "If anyone asks who did it — send them my way. They'll get 10% off their first visit for mentioning you, and you'll get 15% off your next one."

When they mention a friend's hair problem (they do this constantly):

> "Send her to me. First visit's 10% off if she mentions you, and it gets you 15% off your next one."

Once. Lightly. This is a boutique salon — the referral should feel like a favour offered, not a close.

### SMS to clients without email

Short list, high open rate. Send manually, one at a time — a personal text beats a broadcast.

> Hi [Name], it's Sha. I've opened up Thursdays and Fridays — they're my quietest days if you'd like a more relaxed appointment. Also: if you send a friend my way, they get 10% off their first visit and you get 15% off your next. They just mention your name. 0452 611 799

### Google review ask *(after a great appointment, in person)*

> "If you had a good time today, a quick Google review genuinely helps people find me. No stress if not."

Then text the direct review link that evening while it's fresh. In-person ask + same-day link converts far better than either alone.

### Instagram — feed post

Pair with a real result photo (never a graphic-only post).

> Two new days.
>
> I've opened Thursdays and Fridays — my quietest days of the week, which means unhurried appointments and times that were impossible to get before.
>
> And a thank you that's overdue: if you send someone my way, they get 10% off their first visit and you get 15% off your next. No codes, no links — they just mention your name when they book.
>
> Booking link in bio.
>
> #camberwellhair #melbournehairdresser #balayagemelbourne #hairbysha

### Instagram — story sequence (3 frames)

1. Photo of the empty, calm salon → text: *"Thursdays & Fridays are open"*
2. A recent result → text: *"Unhurried appointments. Whole room to yourself."*
3. Text frame → *"Send a friend: they get 10% off, you get 15% off."* + booking link sticker

### Google Business Post

> **New Thursday & Friday appointments available.** I've extended my week at Hair by Sha in Camberwell — Thursdays and Fridays are now open and are the quietest, most unhurried appointments I offer. Balayage, blonde work, colour correction, cuts. Free consultation, no obligation.

## 6. Making it permanent

The referral program should never be switched off. After week 8, it stops being a campaign and becomes standing practice:

- **Every appointment:** the rebooking ask. Non-negotiable.
- **Every great result:** the referral line, once, lightly.
- **Monthly:** one Google Post; refresh Instagram with recent work.
- **Quarterly:** re-send the referral announcement to clients who've joined since the last one. New clients are the most likely to refer — they're still in the enthusiastic phase.
- **Twice yearly:** run the lapsed reactivation email against whoever has drifted past six months.
- **Weekly, five minutes, Monday:** open the tracker. Two questions — who's at `Attended` but hasn't rebooked (text them), and who has referred more than once (those people are the program; look after them).

A referral program's value compounds over quarters, not weeks. The clients it brings in are worth more and stay longer than any other source. Judge it at six months, not at eight weeks.

## 7. Measurement, and what to do when it's off

**The one number:** Thursday and Friday appointments booked per week, tracked in Fresha. Everything else is a leading indicator.

| Leading indicator | Where | Healthy |
|---|---|---|
| Email open rate | Resend | 35–50% |
| Click rate | Resend | 3–8% |
| Referrals named | Tracker | — |
| Referral → attended | Tracker | 50–70% |
| Rebook-at-chair rate | Fresha | 40%+ of appointments |
| Reward redemption | Tracker | 60%+ within 12 weeks |

### Diagnosis

| Symptom | Most likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Thu/Fri still empty, emails opened fine | Rebooking ask isn't happening consistently | Lever 1. This is nearly always the answer. |
| Low open rate (<25%) | Sender name, subject line, or domain auth | Check SPF/DKIM/DMARC first, then send from "Sha —" not the business name |
| Good opens, no referrals | The ask is too abstract | Email 2's "think of one person" is the fix; also make the verbal ask in the chair |
| Referrals named but not attending | Booking friction, or too long a gap | Get them booked while they're keen; send email 4 immediately |
| Rewards never redeemed | Referrer forgot | Email 5 within 24h of attendance is exactly this fix — don't skip it |
| Lots of Thu/Fri bookings but revenue flat | Discounting is eating the gain | Stop discounting beyond the referral offer; fill with rebookings, not offers |

---

## 8. What not to do

- **Don't add a general discount to fill the days.** Discounting trains clients to wait and permanently resets what they think an appointment costs. Fill with rebookings and referrals; use the calendar to steer.
- **Don't fake scarcity.** Email 3 says Thursdays and Fridays are "filling from the front of the day" — only send it if that's true. A boutique salon runs on trust and a single false claim is expensive.
- **Don't email more often than this plan.** Three broadcasts in eight weeks to a personal client list is right. Weekly sends will cost unsubscribes worth more than the bookings.
- **Don't use the site's current testimonials.** They're placeholders. Replace with real Google/Fresha reviews before using social proof anywhere.
- **Don't build an Instagram content strategy to fill two days.** It's the slowest lever with the least control. Keep the profile credible and put the effort into Levers 1 and 2.
- **Don't let the tracker lapse.** An unrecorded referral means a client who did Sha a favour and got nothing back — worse than never running the program.

---

## 9. If the goal changes

If Thursdays and Fridays fill and the constraint moves, the same assets redeploy:

- **Full but revenue too low** → `pricing` and `offers`. The lever is the value of each appointment, not the number.
- **Filling but no-shows** → deposits at booking, plus the pre-appointment expectations already in email 4.
- **New capacity again** → rerun this exact plan, swapping the days in the copy.
- **Want growth beyond word-of-mouth** → Google Business is already warm; local search and the free consultation as a lead magnet are the next step, not paid ads.

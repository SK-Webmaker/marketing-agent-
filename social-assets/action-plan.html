The five habits

If nothing else on this page happens, do these. The first one alone outperforms the entire email and social programme, and costs nothing.

01Rebook in the chair

Before they reach the counter, offer two named times. Never “when suits you?” — that ends in “I'll text you.”

02Set up Google Business Profile

You're eligible as a chair renter. Put signage on your station first — verification depends on it.

03Email two days after

Ask how it's sitting. Fix anything wrong. Then ask for the review.

04Email when colour is due

Two named slots again. Root colour at 4–5 weeks, foils at 8–10, balayage at 10–14.

05Film everything

Phone on a tripod, every colour service. Edit once a week in one 45-minute block.

Before anything goes public

Confirmations and honest limits. Nothing here has been estimated or invented.

Resolved — you're eligible for your own Google listing

Renting a chair inside Hair Hut doesn't block it. You meet Google's practitioner test: clients ask for you by name, you set your own hours and prices. The real risk is Google's local filter hiding whichever listing has fewer reviews — Hair Hut has 57. That's why reviews are urgent, not optional.

Resolved — Kairo is the booking system, and the links are right

All three scheduled emails and both retention templates point at it correctly.

Retention can't be measured yet

Kairo was just installed, so there's no history. A real retention rate needs one full colour cycle, 8–14 weeks. Targets are set forward from now. The lapsed win-back email stays parked until roughly six months of history exists.

Testimonials must be real

The earlier site build carried placeholders explicitly marked for replacement. Don't publish any quote that isn't a real, attributable client.

Confirm “20+ years” and “K18 certified”

Both are on the live site. Certification claims carry real risk if challenged — make sure the documentation exists.

Client consent on before/after photos

Confirm for each image before it goes to social.

# Growing on Returning Clients
**The retention-led growth plan** · Hair by Sha · 29 July 2026

---

## Why this is the right strategy for this business

Sha has **one chair**. That single fact rules out most growth strategies.

She cannot scale by taking more clients — capacity is roughly 20–25 appointments a week, full stop. So growth has to come from one of three places:

| Route | Ceiling | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|
| More clients | Hard-capped by one chair | High cost, high effort |
| Higher prices | Real, but slow and limited | Medium |
| **Same clients, coming back more often** | **Large, and immediate** | **Low — the clients already exist** |

The third is where the growth is, and almost nobody does it deliberately.

## The maths that makes the case

> ⚠️ **This is a model, not a measurement.** Kairo was only just installed, so there is no history to calculate from. The assumptions below are labelled and typical for a colour-led salon. **Replace them with your real numbers as Kairo accumulates data** and the model becomes a forecast.

### Assumptions (state these as estimates, not facts)

| Input | Assumed | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| Active clients | ~110 of the 161 on the list | Low — needs Kairo |
| Average gap between visits | 10 weeks | Low — typical for a mixed colour book |
| Average spend per visit | ⚠️ **you supply** | — |
| Current rebook-in-chair rate | ~25% (industry typical when not asked deliberately) | Low |

### What shortening the gap does

A client on a **10-week** cycle visits **5.2 times a year.**
A client on an **8.5-week** cycle visits **6.1 times a year.**

That is **+0.9 visits per client per year** from changing nothing except when they come back.

Across ~110 active clients:

**≈ 99 additional appointments a year.**

### Compare the two routes to the same result

| To gain ~99 appointments/year | What it takes |
|---|---|
| **Via new clients** | ~19 brand-new clients acquired *and* retained at 5.2 visits each. Months of marketing, referral rewards, discounted first visits. |
| **Via retention** | Ask every existing client to rebook before they leave, and email the ones who don't. **Zero acquisition cost.** |

That is the whole argument. The retention route is roughly **10× cheaper** and starts working the same week.

### The compounding part

New clients from the referral campaign enter the same cycle. A referred client retained on an 8.5-week rhythm is worth **~17% more** over their life than the same client on a 10-week drift. Retention doesn't compete with acquisition — it multiplies it.

## Client segmentation and targets

The 161 contacts are not one group. Treat them as four.

| Segment | Est. size | Definition | Goal | Primary tool |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Regulars** | ~40 | Rebook without prompting | Protect. Do not discount. | In-chair rebooking |
| **Driftors** | ~50 | Come back, but late and irregularly | **Biggest opportunity.** Shorten the gap. | R2 rebooking email |
| **Lapsed** | ~50 | Not seen 6+ months | Win back a fraction | Win-back email *(parked — needs history)* |
| **New / referred** | ~20 | 1–2 visits, habit not formed | Convert to Regular by visit 3 | R1 review email + rebooking |

> ⚠️ **These splits are estimates.** A Kairo export with client + last visit date turns them into real segments in about ten minutes.

### The critical insight about new clients

**Visit three is where a client becomes a regular.** A client who comes twice is still deciding. A client who comes three times has a habit.

So every referred client from the current campaign should be treated as a retention target from the moment they sit down — not an acquisition win to be counted and forgotten. **Getting a new client to visit three is worth more than getting two new clients to visit one.**

## The four things that actually drive retention

Ranked. The first two do most of the work.

### 1. The in-chair rebooking ask — free, immediate, biggest

> "Do you want me to put your next one in now? I've got Thursdays and Fridays open at the moment. I could do **Thursday the 14th at 10:30**, or **Friday the 22nd at 2** — which works better?"

**Two named times, never an open question.** "When suits you?" hands the client homework and reliably ends in "I'll text you," which never arrives.

If Sha sees 18 clients a week and lifts rebooking from 25% to 60%, that is **six extra committed appointments every week** — more than the entire email programme delivers in two months.

### 2. The colour plan, said out loud

> "With this, you'll want me again around week eight. Any longer and the toner starts to go warm on you."

This reframes the visit from a one-off into a **cycle**. It also pre-justifies the rebooking ask, so it doesn't feel like a sale.

### 3. The personal detail

Record one non-hair thing per client — a trip, a job change, a kid's name. Open with it next visit.

In a one-chair salon this is the single strongest loyalty driver. It is also the thing a bigger salon structurally cannot do, which makes it Sha's real moat.

### 4. The emails

R1 (review, 2 days) and R2 (rebooking, at due) — both built and published in Resend. They catch the clients the first three miss.

## Content that supports retention specifically

Most salon content chases new followers. **Retention content has a different job: reminding existing clients that their colour has a clock on it.**

| Content type | Retention job | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **"Your colour at week 4 vs week 9"** | Makes the cycle visible. The single most effective retention post type. | Monthly |
| **Maintenance education** (washing, heat, purple shampoo) | Protects the result, keeps Sha present between visits | Fortnightly |
| **"Booked out for August"** | Scarcity → prompts rebooking now | Monthly |
| **Client-of-the-week before/after** | Regulars see themselves in it | Weekly |
| **Behind the scenes / Sha to camera** | Relationship maintenance between visits | Weekly |

**The highest-performing retention post is the maintenance calendar** — a plain graphic saying *root colour every 5–6 weeks, foils 8–10, balayage 12–14.* It answers the question clients are quietly asking and it directly seeds the rebooking conversation.

## Measuring it

**One number: appointments per week.** Everything else is a leading indicator.

Weekly, 5 minutes, Monday:
1. Appointments last week, and how many rebooked in-chair
2. Who's due this week → send R2
3. Who's overdue → text them
4. Reviews gained

Monthly:
- Rebook rate trend
- Average gap (once data allows)
- Thu/Fri utilisation
- Active client count
